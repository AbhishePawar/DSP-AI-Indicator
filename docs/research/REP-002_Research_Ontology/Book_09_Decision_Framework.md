# Book 09 — Decision Framework

## Purpose

Define the institutional Decision Framework vocabulary that integrates evidence, financial analysis, business quality, management quality, economic moat, risk, valuation, and governance into coherent research conclusions and decision states within DSP AI Indicator.

## Scope

Decision-oriented research meanings as ontology definitions: conclusions, theses, decision confidence, recommendation states, criteria, evidence handling, scenarios, review, revision, lifecycle, and learning. This book does not redefine concepts from Books 01–08 or Book 10. It references those books as dependencies and integration inputs. This book does not define brokerage order routing, portfolio optimizer algorithms, recommendation engine code, API contracts, or user-interface layout.

## Relationship to Prior Books

Book 09 is the integration layer of REP-002. It consumes Book 01 — Core Principles, Book 02 — Research Objects, Book 03 — Financial Ontology, Book 04 — Business Quality, Book 05 — Management, Book 06 — Economic Moat, Book 07 — Risk, Book 08 — Valuation, and Book 10 — Governance & AI Intelligence. Decision Framework concepts must preserve prior definitions and assemble them into decision-ready research meaning.

# Core Definition

## Definition

A research conclusion is an integrated, evidence-based judgment that synthesizes findings across financial analysis, business quality, management, economic moat, risk, and valuation into a stated research outcome for a defined subject and time context. Within DSP AI Indicator, research conclusion is the capstone analytical judgment preceding recommendation state selection.

## Purpose

Research conclusion provides the synthesis lens required to turn multi-domain analysis into a coherent institutional research outcome.

## Why It Matters

Fragmented domain outputs do not equal a decision-ready view. Correct definition ensures conclusions integrate prior books rather than restating any single domain.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

A research conclusion should be:

- Multi-domain integrated
- Evidence-referenced
- Explicit about uncertainty
- Time- and subject-scoped
- Separated from execution instructions
- Revisable under new evidence

## What It Is Not

A research conclusion is not:

- A single metric reading
- A valuation number alone
- A brokerage order
- A redefinition of AI Committee output
- Guaranteed future outcome
- An investment recommendation by itself

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Research conclusions should be supported by:

- Evidence packages across relevant domains
- Financial, quality, management, moat, risk, and valuation inputs as applicable
- Explicit contradictions and unresolved issues
- Confidence labels
- Traceability to sources and decision records where material

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Domains converge.
- Contradictions are resolved or disclosed.
- Evidence is current and complete.
- Confidence is honestly labeled.
- Governance validation gates pass.

## Validation

Research conclusions are validated through:

- Multi-domain coverage checks
- Contradiction disclosure review
- Traceability resolution
- Confidence labeling review
- Policy conformance
- Separation from recommendation-state identity

# Research Guidance

## Research Implication

Material research outputs shall state a research conclusion that integrates relevant domains and discloses residual uncertainty rather than presenting isolated scores as conclusions.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Durable franchise with adequate valuation cushion and manageable risk
- High quality business impaired by governance and leverage risk
- Attractive multiple undermined by moat erosion evidence
- Conclusion deferred due to incomplete filings
- Single-ratio signal treated as full conclusion

## Limitations

Conclusions remain judgments under uncertainty. Integration quality depends on evidence completeness and honest contradiction handling.

# Core Definition

## Definition

An investment thesis is a structured narrative of why a security or organization may create or destroy owner value over a stated horizon, specifying the core drivers, key assumptions, principal risks, invalidation conditions, and required evidence. Within DSP AI Indicator, investment thesis is the decision-framework concept that organizes research into a testable value story.

## Purpose

Investment thesis provides the structured-story lens required to make research decisions falsifiable and reviewable.

## Why It Matters

Without a thesis, analysis becomes a pile of observations. Correct definition forces clarity on drivers, risks, and what would prove the thesis wrong.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

An investment thesis should be:

- Driver-explicit
- Assumption-explicit
- Risk-aware
- Horizon-scoped
- Invalidation-ready
- Evidence-linked

## What It Is Not

An investment thesis is not:

- A slogan or ticker narrative
- A valuation model by itself
- A recommendation state by itself
- Guaranteed alpha
- A redefinition of Economic Moat or Business Quality
- An investment recommendation by mere existence

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Investment theses should be supported by:

- Stated value drivers tied to prior-book concepts
- Key assumptions with confidence notes
- Principal risks referencing Book 07
- Invalidation conditions
- Supporting evidence references
- Time horizon statement

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Drivers are specific and evidenced.
- Invalidation conditions are crisp.
- Risks are complete relative to the story.
- Assumptions are conservative and disclosed.
- Independent review finds coherence.

## Validation

Investment theses are validated through:

- Driver-to-evidence linkage checks
- Invalidation completeness review
- Risk coverage review
- Assumption disclosure checks
- Horizon clarity tests
- Separation from recommendation-state identity

## Research Implication

Material long-horizon research shall maintain an investment thesis with explicit invalidation conditions and linked evidence.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Compounder thesis based on reinvestment and durable moat
- Mean-reversion thesis based on temporary earnings depression
- Avoid thesis based on integrity and leverage risk

## Limitations

A clear thesis can still be wrong. Thesis quality is about structure and falsifiability, not inevitability.

# Core Definition

## Definition

Decision confidence is the assessed reliability of a research conclusion or decision state after integrating multi-domain evidence and residual uncertainty, applying CP-007 Confidence and GV-004 Analytical Confidence Level without redefining them. Within DSP AI Indicator, decision confidence is the decision-framework confidence used at the point of research decisioning.

## Purpose

Decision confidence provides the go/no-go reliability lens required before selecting or revising a recommendation state.

## Why It Matters

Acting on low-confidence conclusions creates avoidable permanent capital loss risk. Correct definition binds decisioning to honest confidence communication.

---

Decision confidence should be:

- Integration-aware across domains
- Explicitly labeled

- Distinct from recommendation aggressiveness

- A redefinition of CP-007 Confidence

- A recommendation state
- Market consensus
- An investment recommendation

# Core Definition

## Definition

A recommendation state is the governed categorical stance assigned to a research subject under applicable research policy—such as watch, accumulate interest, hold posture, reduce posture, or avoid posture—derived from a research conclusion and decision confidence. Within DSP AI Indicator, recommendation state applies GV-003 Recommendation without redefining it; it specifies the decision-framework state machine for stance presentation.

## Purpose

Recommendation state provides the stance-category lens required to translate integrated conclusions into policy-compatible research positions.

## Why It Matters

Users need clear stances, but stances must remain gated and explainable. Correct definition separates stance categories from the broader recommendation governance concept.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

A recommendation state should be:

- Categorical and labeled
- Policy-gated
- Conclusion-derived
- Confidence-qualified
- Time-stamped and revisable
- Non-executing by itself

## What It Is Not

A recommendation state is not:

- A redefinition of GV-003 Recommendation
- A brokerage order
- Guaranteed performance
- A valuation result
- Hidden score without label
- Unrestricted advice under Research Mode defaults

# Core Definition

## Definition

Evidence weighting is the governed practice of assigning relative importance to evidence items based on source reliability, relevance, recency, corroboration, and claim category when forming research conclusions. Within DSP AI Indicator, evidence weighting applies Book 01 evidence principles and Book 02 source concepts without redefining them.

## Purpose

Evidence weighting provides the prioritization lens required to integrate unequal evidence quality into coherent conclusions.

## Why It Matters

Treating all evidence as equal produces distorted conclusions. Correct definition makes weighting explicit and challengeable.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Evidence weighting should be:

- Explicit where material
- Source-quality aware
- Corroboration-sensitive
- Recency-aware
- Category-honest
- Revisable

## What It Is Not

Evidence weighting is not:

- Fabricating missing evidence
- Ignoring inconvenient facts
- A redefinition of CP-002 Evidence
- A redefinition of RO-007 Source
- A valuation method
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Evidence weighting should be supported by:

- Evidence inventory
- Source reliability notes
- Corroboration status
- Recency and relevance tags
- Weighting rationale for material items
- Change log when weights shift

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- High-reliability sources dominate.
- Independent corroboration exists.
- Weighting rationale is disclosed.
- Stale evidence is down-weighted.
- Independent review agrees.

## Validation

Evidence weighting is validated through:

- Rationale disclosure checks
- Source-reliability consistency
- Anti-cherry-picking review
- Traceability of weighted items
- Revision logging
- Sampling audits

# Core Definition

## Definition

Contradictory evidence handling is the governed process for detecting, disclosing, weighing, and resolving or deferring conflicts among evidence items before a research conclusion or recommendation state is finalized. Within DSP AI Indicator, contradictory evidence handling protects truthfulness in integrated decisioning.

## Purpose

Contradictory evidence handling provides the conflict-resolution lens required to prevent silent suppression of inconvenient facts.

## Why It Matters

Hidden contradictions destroy trust and increase permanent capital loss risk. Correct definition forces conflicts into the open.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Contradictory evidence handling should be:

- Detection-oriented
- Disclosure-mandatory
- Resolution-or-deferral explicit
- Weighting-aware
- Audit-logged
- Confidence-impacting

## What It Is Not

Contradictory evidence handling is not:

- Choosing the convenient side quietly
- Averaging incompatible claims without rationale
- A valuation method
- Proof that all conflicts can be resolved
- A recommendation state
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

Contradictory evidence handling should be supported by:

- Identified conflicting items
- Nature of conflict
- Weighting and reliability comparison
- Resolution rationale or deferral reason
- Impact on decision confidence

- Conflicts are surfaced early.
- Resolution logic is transparent.
- Deferrals are used when evidence is insufficient.
- Confidence is reduced appropriately.

## Validation

Contradictory evidence handling is validated through:

- Conflict-detection sampling
- Disclosure completeness checks
- Resolution rationale review
- Confidence-impact consistency
- Anti-suppression audits

# Core Definition

## Definition

Scenario analysis is the structured examination of multiple plausible future states—typically including adverse, base, and favorable cases—to test how research conclusions, valuation outcomes, and risks change under different assumptions. Within DSP AI Indicator, scenario analysis is a decision-framework method for uncertainty exploration, not a prediction claim.

## Purpose

Scenario analysis provides the multi-future lens required to stress theses and valuation under uncertainty.

## Why It Matters

Single-point forecasts hide fragility. Correct definition supports robust decisioning and links to valuation confidence and risk concepts without redefining them.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Scenario analysis should be:

- Assumption-explicit
- Plausibility-bounded
- Comparative across cases
- Linked to thesis drivers and risks
- Confidence-informing
- Non-prophetic in presentation

## What It Is Not

Scenario analysis is not:

- A guaranteed forecast
- Proof of one true future
- A substitute for evidence
- A redefinition of Terminal Value
- A recommendation state
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Scenario analysis should be supported by:

- Defined scenario set
- Key assumption differences
- Linked domain impacts (financials, moat, risk, valuation)
- Probability language only if justified and disclosed
- Sensitivity highlights
- Implication for decision confidence

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Scenarios span material uncertainties.
- Assumptions are coherent per case.
- Adverse cases are serious, not token.
- Valuation and risk impacts are shown.
- Independent review finds usefulness.

## Validation

Scenario analysis is validated through:

- Assumption disclosure checks
- Adverse-case seriousness review
- Cross-domain impact completeness
- Anti-false-precision screening
- Linkage to decision confidence
- Documentation sufficiency
