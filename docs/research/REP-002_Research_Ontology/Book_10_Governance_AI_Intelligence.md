# Book 10 — Governance & AI Intelligence

## Purpose

Define the institutional Governance and AI Intelligence vocabulary used to govern, validate, explain, audit, and present analytical intelligence across DSP AI Indicator.

## Scope

Platform governance, AI committee, explainability, recommendation presentation, confidence communication, traceability, auditability, validation, and human oversight meanings as ontology definitions. This book does not redefine Core Principles, Research Objects, Financial Ontology, Business Quality, Management, Economic Moat, Risk, or Valuation concepts. Management Transparency (MQ-007) and general Confidence (CP-007) remain owned by their books; this book defines research-intelligence presentation and governance applications. This book does not define model weights, prompt text, API contracts, UI layout, or recommendation algorithms.

## Relationship to Prior Books

Book 10 consumes Books 01–08. Governance and AI Intelligence concepts must preserve Truth, Evidence, Fact, Assumption, Inference, and Confidence distinctions; remain anchored to research objects; and consume financial, quality, management, moat, risk, and valuation meanings by reference only.

# Core Definition

## Definition

The AI Committee is the governed multi-perspective analytical review process through which DSP AI Indicator synthesizes evidence, competing interpretations, risk, quality, and valuation context into structured intelligence outputs under explicit rules and human-oversight constraints. Within DSP AI Indicator, AI Committee is a foundational governance concept for institutional AI reasoning presentation.

## Purpose

AI Committee provides the multi-perspective review lens required to prevent single-model opacity and to structure analytical challenge before intelligence is presented.

## Why It Matters

High-stakes research intelligence requires contested review, not unchecked generation. Correct definition enables consistent AI Committee semantics for explainability, auditability, and trust.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

AI Committee should be:

- Multi-perspective
- Rule-governed
- Evidence-referenced
- Explicit about disagreement
- Separated from final human decision authority
- Auditable

## What It Is Not

AI Committee is not:

- A single opaque model answer
- A human investment committee substitute by default
- A valuation method
- A guarantee of correctness
- An unsupervised trading agent
- An investment recommendation by mere existence

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

AI Committee operation should be supported by:

- Input evidence packages and source references
- Perspective or role definitions
- Governance rules applied
- Disagreement and concurrence records
- Confidence labels
- Human oversight checkpoints where required

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Evidence coverage is broad.
- Perspectives challenge each other.
- Rules are explicit and applied.
- Uncertainty is disclosed.
- Oversight gates are intact.

## Validation

AI Committee outputs are validated through:

- Evidence linkage checks
- Rule-application review
- Disagreement capture tests
- Confidence labeling review
- Oversight-gate confirmation
- Audit-trail completeness

# Research Guidance

## Research Implication

AI Committee outputs shall be presented as governed analytical synthesis with evidence links, disagreement notes, and confidence labels—not as unexplained authority.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Multi-agent review of quality, risk, and valuation perspectives
- Recorded dissent on moat durability
- Committee output blocked pending missing evidence
- Single-model summary labeled as committee output
- Human override of a committee draft recommendation

## Limitations

Committee process improves structure and challenge but cannot eliminate uncertainty or guarantee correct conclusions.

# Core Definition

## Definition

Explainability is the capability and practice of presenting analytical outputs so that users can understand what was concluded, why it was concluded, why it matters, and what investigation step follows, with clear separation of evidence, calculation, inference, and opinion. Within DSP AI Indicator, explainability is a core governance concept for trust-compatible intelligence presentation.

## Purpose

Explainability provides the understanding lens required to make analytical intelligence inspectable and challengeable.

## Why It Matters

Unexplained intelligence undermines trust. Correct definition operationalizes User Trust Standard expectations for What / Why / Why care / What next without redefining Core Principles.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Explainability should be:

- User-comprehensible
- Evidence-linked
- Layer-separated (data, calculation, inference, opinion)
- Actionable with a next step
- Consistent in terminology
- Non-manipulative

## What It Is Not

Explainability is not:

- Marketing narrative
- Post-hoc rationalization without evidence
- Full model-weight disclosure by mandate
- A substitute for correctness
- A recommendation by itself
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Explainability should be supported by:

- Traceable evidence references
- Explicit reasoning steps or factors
- Separation of categories of claim
- Confidence and limitation statements
- Next investigation actions
- Consistent ontology terminology

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Explanations match underlying evidence.
- Claim categories are honest.
- Key drivers are specific.
- Limitations are disclosed.
- Users can verify links.

## Validation

Explainability is validated through:

- Evidence-link integrity checks
- Layer-separation review
- Terminology consistency tests
- Next-step presence checks
- Source confirmation
- Anti-fabrication screening

# Core Definition

## Definition

A recommendation is a governed, explicitly labeled analytical suggestion about a possible research or investment action stance, presented only under applicable policy, feature flags, and compliance constraints, and always accompanied by evidence, confidence, and limitations. Within DSP AI Indicator, recommendation is a governance concept for controlled suggestion presentation—not an unsupervised order to trade.

## Purpose

Recommendation provides the controlled-suggestion lens required to separate research education from action suggestions under Research Mode and compliance gates.

## Why It Matters

Unmanaged recommendations create trust and compliance risk. Correct definition keeps recommendations policy-bound, explainable, and distinct from raw analysis.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

A recommendation should be:

- Policy-gated
- Explicitly labeled
- Evidence-supported
- Confidence-qualified
- Reversible under new evidence
- Separated from execution instructions

## What It Is Not

A recommendation is not:

- A brokerage order
- Guaranteed outcome
- A substitute for user judgment
- Hidden inside unexplained scores
- Valuation by itself
- Unrestricted advice in Research Mode defaults

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Recommendations should be supported by:

- Underlying analysis artefacts
- Explicit decision factors
- Applicable policy and flag state
- Confidence and risk caveats
- Explainability content
- Timestamp and version context

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Evidence is strong and current.
- Policies are satisfied.
- Caveats are complete.
- Explainability is intact.
- Oversight requirements are met.

## Validation

Recommendations are validated through:

- Policy-gate checks
- Evidence linkage review
- Labeling and caveat completeness
- Feature-flag compliance
- Audit-trail presence
- Separation from execution commands

Recommendations shall appear only when policy permits and shall never be presented without evidence basis, confidence, and limitations.

Analytical confidence level is the governed presentation of how much trust should be placed in a specific analytical output, applying CP-007 Confidence to intelligence surfaces without redefining CP-007. Within DSP AI Indicator, analytical confidence level is a governance concept for consistent confidence communication across AI Committee, reports, and explainability.

## Purpose

Analytical confidence level provides the presentation lens required to communicate reliability of intelligence outputs honestly and consistently.

## Why It Matters

Users need calibrated trust signals. Correct definition prevents silent certainty and keeps confidence semantics aligned with Core Principles.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Analytical confidence level should be:

- Explicitly displayed
- Evidence-sensitive
- Output-specific
- Comparable under shared criteria
- Distinct from recommendation strength
- Updateable

## What It Is Not

Analytical confidence level is not:

- A redefinition of CP-007 Confidence
- Proof of correctness
- Market consensus
- A recommendation
- Valuation confidence by itself (VC-012 remains distinct)
- An investment recommendation

---

Analytical confidence levels should be supported by:

- Evidence completeness indicators
- Assumption uncertainty notes
- Method suitability context
- Cross-check agreement or divergence
- Known limitations
- Timestamp of assessment

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Evidence is strong and triangulated.
- Methods agree.
- Assumptions are conservative and disclosed.
- Blind spots are small.
- Independent review concurs.

## Validation

Analytical confidence levels are validated through:

- Mapping integrity to CP-007 practice
- Evidence-strength review
- Label consistency checks
- Source confirmation
- Separation from recommendation labels
- Refresh-on-new-evidence tests

## Research Implication

Intelligence platforms shall retain the minimum record set required for independent process audit of material outputs.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Auditor reconstructs why a recommendation was gated
- Missing exception logs blocking audit
- Complete committee packet with versions and approvals
- Immutable storage of decision records
- Ad hoc chat output with no retainable trail

## Limitations

Retention and privacy constraints can limit audit depth. Auditability of process is not the same as proof that a market outcome was predicted correctly.

# Core Definition

## Definition

A decision record is a durable, structured record of a material analytical or governance decision, including context, inputs, rules applied, outcome, confidence, responsible parties, and timestamp. Within DSP AI Indicator, decision records are core artefacts for accountability and institutional memory.

## Purpose

Decision records provide the durable-memory lens required to preserve why material intelligence actions were taken or withheld.

## Why It Matters

Without decision records, organizations repeat errors and cannot explain past outputs. Correct definition supports auditability and learning.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

A decision record should be:

- Durable
- Structured
- Timestamped
- Input-referenced
- Outcome-explicit
- Attribution-aware

## What It Is Not

A decision record is not:

- An informal chat message alone
- A full raw model log dump by default
- A recommendation itself
- A valuation method
- Ephemeral UI state
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Decision records should be supported by:

- Decision identifier
- Context and triggering event
- Inputs and evidence references
- Rules and policies applied
- Outcome and rationale summary
- Actor or system attribution and time

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Records are complete and promptly written.
- References resolve.
- Attribution is clear.
- Amendments are versioned.
- Independent retrieval succeeds.

## Validation

Decision records are validated through:

- Completeness schema checks
- Reference resolution
- Amendment-history integrity
- Retention compliance
- Attribution presence
- Sampling audits

Examples include:

- Human override approving release with rationale
- Committee dissent captured in decision packet

# Core Definition

## Definition

A validation rule is an explicit check that determines whether an analytical input, intermediate result, or output satisfies required evidence, consistency, completeness, or policy conditions before further use or presentation. Within DSP AI Indicator, validation rules are core governance controls for quality gating.

## Purpose

Validation rules provide the acceptance-check lens required to prevent invalid or incomplete intelligence from propagating.

## Why It Matters

Ungated outputs create silent failures. Correct definition standardizes validation as a first-class governance concept distinct from valuation or risk models.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

A validation rule should be:

- Explicit and testable
- Pass/fail or graded with clear criteria
- Scoped to inputs or outputs
- Logged on failure
- Versioned
- Policy-aligned

## What It Is Not

A validation rule is not:

- A valuation model
- A business-quality score
- A recommendation
- An informal reviewer preference
- A substitute for human judgment in all cases
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Validation rules should be supported by:

- Rule criteria text
- Applicable stage in the pipeline
- Pass/fail logs
- Failure reasons
- Version identity
- Remediation path

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Criteria are objective and clear.
- Failures are actionable.
- Bypass is rare and authorized.
- Coverage of critical paths is high.
- Independent tests confirm enforcement.

## Validation

Validation rules are validated through:

- Unit and integration enforcement tests
- Failure-log completeness
- Bypass-authorization review
- Coverage analysis of critical outputs
- Version control checks
- Periodic criterion review

# Research Guidance

## Research Implication

Critical intelligence pathways shall apply validation rules before presentation, with failures visible to operators and logged for audit.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Reject incomplete financial statement packages
- Require currency and period labels on metrics
- Block recommendation without confidence label
- Soft-warn on mild peer outliers
- Silent acceptance of missing mandatory fields

## Limitations

Validation rules catch many defects but cannot detect all conceptual errors or future information that has not yet arrived.

# Core Definition

## Definition

Human oversight is the governed requirement that accountable persons review, approve, challenge, or override analytical intelligence in defined circumstances, preserving human responsibility for material research and advice-related outcomes. Within DSP AI Indicator, human oversight is a core governance concept for safe AI deployment.

## Purpose

Human oversight provides the accountability lens required to keep humans responsible for material intelligence actions.

## Why It Matters

AI systems can scale errors as easily as insights. Correct definition ensures oversight is real, logged, and proportionate to risk.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Human oversight should be:

- Role-accountable
- Trigger-defined
- Documented in decision records
- Challenge-capable
- Proportionate to risk and policy
- Non-ceremonial

## What It Is Not

Human oversight is not:

- A rubber stamp
- Full manual recreation of every calculation
- Elimination of AI assistance
- A recommendation by itself
- Diffused unaccountable review
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Human oversight should be supported by:

- Oversight role definitions
- Trigger conditions
- Reviewer identity and time
- Approval or rejection outcome
- Rationale summary
- Link to decision record

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Triggers are enforced.
- Reviewers are competent and independent where required.
- Outcomes are logged.
- Overrides are explained.
- Sampling shows non-ceremonial review.

## Validation

Human oversight is validated through:

- Trigger-enforcement tests
- Decision-record completeness
- Role-authorization checks
- Override-rationale review
- Periodic quality sampling
- Policy conformance audits

# Research Guidance

## Research Implication

Material recommendation unlocks, high-uncertainty releases, and policy exceptions shall require human oversight with retained decision records.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Analyst approval before advice-mode recommendation publish
- Oversight rejection of unsupported AI narrative
- Automatic low-risk research summary without manual gate
- Unlogged override of a validation failure
- Periodic sampling of committee outputs by seniors

## Limitations

Human oversight quality varies with reviewer skill and time pressure. Oversight reduces but does not eliminate error.
