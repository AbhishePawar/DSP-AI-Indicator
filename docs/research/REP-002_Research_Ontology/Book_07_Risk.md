# Book 07 — Risk

## Purpose

Define the institutional Risk vocabulary used to identify, classify, and communicate exposures that can impair business durability, financial resilience, portfolio outcomes, and long-term owner capital within DSP AI Indicator.

## Scope

Risk classes and risk-mitigation meanings as ontology definitions. This book does not redefine Business Quality, Management, Economic Moat, or valuation methodologies. Margin of Safety is defined here strictly as a risk-mitigation concept and not as a valuation method. This book does not define risk-engine algorithms, portfolio optimisers, position-sizing formulas, recommendation logic, API contracts, or user-interface presentation.

## Relationship to Prior Books

Book 07 consumes Book 01 — Core Principles, Book 02 — Research Objects, Book 03 — Financial Ontology, Book 04 — Business Quality, Book 05 — Management, and Book 06 — Economic Moat. Risk concepts must preserve epistemological distinctions from Book 01, remain anchored to research objects, interpret financial evidence using Book 03, and reference Business Quality, Management, and Economic Moat concepts without redefining them. Valuation methodology remains outside this book.

# Core Definition

## Definition

Business risk is the exposure of an enterprise to adverse outcomes arising from its operating model, competitive environment, demand variability, cost structure, and strategic choices. Within DSP AI Indicator, business risk is a foundational risk concept used to assess the fragility of operating earnings and franchise durability independent of capital structure.

## Purpose

Business risk provides the operating-exposure lens required to judge how vulnerable a business is to competitive, demand, and cost shocks.

## Why It Matters

Even strong franchises can be impaired by high operating fragility. Correctly defining business risk enables consistent Buffett-style caution, AI Committee reasoning, and explainability of operating vulnerability without confusing it with financial leverage or valuation.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Business risk should be:

- Rooted in operating and competitive exposures
- Distinct from financing structure
- Observable in earnings and cash volatility
- Sensitive to industry and demand shocks
- Linked to cost flexibility and pricing power
- Evidence-based

## What It Is Not

Business risk is not:

- Financial risk by itself
- Economic Moat absence alone
- Management quality
- A valuation multiple
- Temporary stock-price volatility
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Business risk assessments should be supported by:

- Earnings and cash-flow volatility
- Competitive intensity evidence
- Demand cyclicality and customer concentration
- Cost structure flexibility
- Pricing power and margin resilience
- Multi-period operating stress outcomes

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Operating exposures are clearly identified.
- Stress history is observable.
- Competitive threats are documented.
- Cost flexibility is understood.
- Independent evidence agrees.

## Validation

Business risk is validated through:

- Operating-exposure review
- Cycle-stress evidence checks
- Separation from leverage effects
- Source confirmation
- Peer and industry context
- Persistence of risk factors

# Research Guidance

## Research Implication

Analytical engines and research narratives shall treat business risk as operating and competitive exposure, not as a synonym for stock volatility or weak recent sentiment.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Highly cyclical industrial demand
- Commodity price-exposed producer
- Stable subscription franchise with low churn
- Fashion-driven product risk
- Concentrated customer dependency

## Limitations

Business risk is partly judgmental and dynamic. Historical stability does not eliminate future competitive or demand shocks.

# Core Definition

## Definition

Financial risk is the exposure of an enterprise to adverse outcomes arising from capital structure, debt service obligations, refinancing needs, liquidity shortfalls, and related financial commitments. Within DSP AI Indicator, financial risk is a core risk concept used to assess balance-sheet and financing fragility.

## Purpose

Financial risk provides the capital-structure exposure lens required to judge whether leverage and financing choices threaten solvency or owner value.

## Why It Matters

High business quality can still fail under excessive financial risk. Correct definition enables consistent leverage and coverage analysis while referencing Book 03 financial concepts without redefining them.

---

Financial risk should be:

- Linked to debt, obligations, and liquidity
- Distinct from operating business risk
- Observable in leverage and coverage metrics
- Sensitive to refinancing and interest conditions
- Interpretable with cash generation
- Evidence-based

## What It Is Not

- Business risk by itself
- Currency risk by itself
- Temporary earnings noise
- A credit rating by itself
- A valuation multiple
- An investment recommendation

## Definition

Industry risk is the exposure shared by participants in an industry due to structural forces such as rivalry, cyclicality, technological change, substitution, regulation, and input-market instability. Within DSP AI Indicator, industry risk is a core risk concept used to contextualize company-level risk inside industry conditions.

## Purpose

Industry risk provides the sector-environment exposure lens required to judge whether adverse industry forces elevate risk for all or most participants.

## Why It Matters

Strong operators can still be impaired by hostile industry structure. Correct definition references BQ-007 Industry Structure without redefining it and keeps risk analysis environmentally grounded.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Industry risk should be:

- Shared across relevant peers
- Structure-linked
- Distinct from company-specific execution failure
- Sensitive to cycles and disruption
- Dynamic over time
- Evidence-based

## What It Is Not

Industry risk is not:

- A redefinition of BQ-007 Industry Structure
- Company-specific operational incidents alone
- Financial leverage
- A valuation multiple
- Temporary sentiment toward a sector
- An investment recommendation

---

Industry risk assessments should be supported by:

- Industry margin and return patterns
- Rivalry and entry dynamics

- Regulatory regime characteristics

- Industry boundaries are clear.

## Validation

Industry risk is validated through:

Within DSP AI Indicator, concentration risk is a core risk concept used in both security research and portfolio intelligence.

## Purpose

Concentration risk provides the dependency-concentration lens required to judge whether outcomes are overly reliant on few nodes.

## Why It Matters

Concentrated dependencies amplify loss severity when a single relationship, region, or holding fails. Correct definition supports research caution and portfolio construction discipline.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Concentration risk should be:

- Dependency-weighted
- Applicable at company and portfolio levels
- Distinct from diversification slogans
- Sensitive to single-point failure
- Quantifiable where disclosures allow
- Evidence-based

## What It Is Not

Concentration risk is not:

- Any focused strategy automatically
- Industry risk by itself
- Liquidity risk by itself
- A valuation multiple
- Temporary promotional customer wins alone
- An investment recommendation

## Validation

Within DSP AI Indicator, margin of safety is defined here strictly as a risk-mitigation concept and not as a valuation method or pricing formula.

## Purpose

Margin of safety provides the protective-buffer lens required to judge whether research and portfolio decisions leave room for error against risk exposures.

## Why It Matters

Uncertainty is unavoidable in investment research. Correctly defining margin of safety as risk mitigation supports Buffett-style caution, position sizing, and explainability without importing valuation methodology into this book.

Margin of safety shall be applied as a protective response to uncertainty and downside risk. Any numerical valuation discount practices belong to valuation ontology and engines, not to this risk definition.
