# Book 06 — Economic Moat

## Purpose

Define the institutional Economic Moat vocabulary used to identify, classify, and assess durable competitive advantages that enable businesses to earn superior returns over long periods within DSP AI Indicator.

## Scope

Economic Moat meanings and moat-source attributes as ontology definitions. This book does not redefine Business Quality concepts from Book 04, Management concepts from Book 05, or valuation methodologies from Book 08. Where Cost Advantage and Scale Advantage are discussed as moat sources, their underlying attribute meanings remain owned by BQ-005 and BQ-006; this book defines their role as sources of economic moat. This book does not define scoring algorithms, recommendation logic, API contracts, or user-interface presentation.

Book 06 consumes Book 01 — Core Principles, Book 02 — Research Objects, Book 03 — Financial Ontology, Book 04 — Business Quality, and Book 05 — Management. Moat concepts must preserve epistemological distinctions from Book 01, remain anchored to research objects, interpret financial evidence using Book 03, and reference Business Quality and Management concepts without redefining them. Valuation remains outside this book.

# Core Definition

## Definition

An economic moat is a durable competitive advantage that protects a business’s ability to earn returns above its cost of capital for an extended period by impeding rival encroachment. Within DSP AI Indicator, economic moat is a foundational analytical concept used in Buffett-style research to judge the persistence of superior economics.

## Purpose

Economic moat provides the institutional framework required to distinguish temporary outperformance from structurally protected advantage.

## Why It Matters

Long-term investment research depends on whether superior returns can persist. Correctly defining economic moat enables consistent competitive-advantage analysis, AI Committee reasoning, explainability, and portfolio intelligence without confusing moat with valuation or management personality.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

An economic moat should be:

- Durable over multi-year horizons
- Structural rather than temporary
- Linked to identifiable advantage sources
- Observable in protected economics
- Sensitive to erosion threats
- Separated from market price

## What It Is Not

An economic moat is not:

- Business Quality as a whole
- Management quality
- A high stock price
- A valuation multiple
- Short-term product popularity
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Economic moat assessments should be supported by:

- Persistent returns on capital
- Identifiable advantage mechanisms
- Competitor and industry evidence
- Customer and pricing durability
- Barriers to imitation or substitution
- Multi-period margin and share resilience

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Advantage mechanisms are clear.
- Superior returns persist across cycles.
- Rivals fail to close the gap.
- Erosion threats are limited.
- Independent evidence agrees.

## Validation

Economic moats are validated through:

- Mechanism identification
- Return persistence review
- Rival response evidence
- Erosion-signal screening
- Source confirmation
- Separation from price narratives

# Research Guidance

## Research Implication

Analytical engines and research narratives shall treat economic moat as a structured judgment about protected advantage and return persistence, not as a synonym for recent profitability or favorable market sentiment.

## Examples

Examples include:

- Brand-protected consumer franchise
- Marketplace with strong network effects
- High switching-cost enterprise software
- Regulated essential service with durable barriers
- Cyclical winner with no structural protection

## Limitations

Economic moat is partly judgmental and can decay. Presence of a moat does not determine purchase price or guarantee future returns.

## Purpose

Brand strength provides the brand-based advantage lens required to judge whether customer preference creates protected economics.

## Why It Matters

Strong brands can sustain pricing and loyalty through cycles. Correct definition prevents equating advertising spend or awareness with a durable moat.

- Marketing budget size

- An investment recommendation

# Core Definition

## Definition

Network effects exist when the value of a product or platform to each user increases as more users, participants, or complementary nodes join the network. Within DSP AI Indicator, network effects are a moat-source concept used to assess self-reinforcing demand-side advantages.

## Purpose

Network effects provide the participation-scale lens required to judge whether growth itself strengthens competitive protection.

## Why It Matters

True network effects can create powerful winner-take-most dynamics. Correct definition distinguishes genuine network value from ordinary scale or distribution reach.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Network effects should be:

- Value-increasing with participation
- Difficult for smaller networks to match
- Observable in engagement or liquidity
- Potentially direct, indirect, or data-driven
- Distinct from mere user count growth
- Evidence-based

## What It Is Not

Network effects are not:

- Any large user base
- Brand strength alone
- Distribution advantage alone
- Temporary viral growth
- A valuation multiple
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Network effects assessments should be supported by:

- Participation and density metrics
- Liquidity or matching quality evidence
- Cross-side complementary value
- Winner-take-most competitive patterns
- Retention improvements with scale
- Failed rival catch-up attempts

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Value clearly rises with network size.
- Multihoming is limited.
- Density advantages compound.
- Rivals struggle despite capital.
- Independent evidence agrees.

## Validation

Network effects are validated through:

- Value-to-participation linkage review
- Multihoming and churn screening
- Rival catch-up evidence
- Source confirmation
- Persistence checks
- Distinction from ordinary scale

# Core Definition

## Definition

Switching costs are the economic, operational, procedural, or relational costs customers incur when moving from one provider to a rival, thereby discouraging defection. Within DSP AI Indicator, switching costs are a moat-source concept used to assess retention-based competitive protection.

## Purpose

Switching costs provide the retention-barrier lens required to judge whether customer relationships are structurally sticky.

## Why It Matters

High switching costs support pricing power and revenue durability. Correct definition prevents confusing contractual lock-in optics with true economic switching friction, and references BQ-004 Customer Stickiness without redefining it.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Switching costs should be:

- Material to customer decisions
- Durable beyond short contracts
- Linked to integration, learning, data, or risk
- Observable in retention behavior
- Distinct from temporary promotions
- Evidence-based

## What It Is Not

Switching costs are not:

- Customer stickiness as a whole (BQ-004)
- Brand preference alone
- Illegal lock-in
- One-year contracts by themselves
- A valuation multiple
- An investment recommendation

---

# Evidence

## Evidence Requirements

Switching cost assessments should be supported by:

- Integration and workflow dependence
- Data migration difficulty
- Retraining and process redesign costs
- Retention and churn patterns
- Multi-product embedding
- Customer testimony or disclosure evidence

## Confidence Drivers

Confidence increases when:

- Defection is rare despite price gaps.
- Migration is operationally painful.
- Embedded workflows are deep.
- Customers accept price increases.
- Independent evidence agrees.

## Validation

Switching costs are validated through:

- Mechanism specificity review
- Retention linkage checks
- Contract-only screening
- Source confirmation
- Competitive displacement evidence
- Persistence across product generations

# Core Definition

## Definition

Within DSP AI Indicator, cost-based moat applies BQ-005 as a moat source and does not redefine Cost Advantage.

## Purpose

Cost-based moat provides the cost-protection lens required to judge whether low-cost economics create lasting competitive insulation.

## Why It Matters

In many industries, cost leadership is the primary durable advantage. Correct definition keeps Business Quality attribute meaning in Book 04 while enabling moat-specific analysis here.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

A cost-based moat should be:

- Rooted in structural cost advantage (BQ-005)
- Durable against rival imitation
- Economically decisive in competition
- Visible in pricing or margin defense
- Distinct from temporary cost windfalls
- Evidence-based

## What It Is Not

A cost-based moat is not:

- A redefinition of BQ-005 Cost Advantage
- Temporary input-cost luck
- Underinvestment disguised as efficiency
- Scale-based moat by itself
- A valuation multiple
- An investment recommendation

---

- Evidence meeting BQ-005 requirements
- Persistence of unit-cost gaps
- Rival inability to match costs

- Multi-period competitive outcomes

## Confidence Drivers

- Cost gaps persist across cycles.
- Causes are structural and hard to copy.
- Quality is not sacrificed unsustainably.
- Rivals fail despite investment.

## Validation

Cost-based moats are validated through:

Within DSP AI Indicator, intangible assets are a moat-source concept used to assess non-physical barriers and advantage repositories.

## Purpose

Intangible assets provide the non-physical advantage lens required to judge whether protected knowledge, rights, or identity sustain superior economics.

Many modern franchises are protected by intangibles rather than plant and equipment. Correct definition supports Buffett-style analysis of invisible but economically decisive advantages.

- Difficult to replicate quickly
- Legally or practically defensible where applicable

- Difficult for smaller rivals to close

- A redefinition of BQ-006 Scale Advantage
- Large revenue alone

- Temporary share spikes
- A valuation multiple
- An investment recommendation

# Core Definition

## Definition

Moat erosion is the weakening or loss of previously protective competitive advantages, observable through diminishing barriers, intensifying rivalry, falling pricing power, or declining return persistence. Within DSP AI Indicator, moat erosion is a core Economic Moat concept used to challenge stale moat judgments and trigger research review.

## Purpose

Moat erosion provides the early-warning vocabulary required to keep economic-moat assessments current and intellectually honest.

## Why It Matters

Moats can decay before earnings fully collapse. Correct definition supports monitoring, AI Committee caution, explainability of downgrades, and portfolio risk awareness, while remaining distinct from BQ-012 Quality Deterioration Signal.

---

# Characteristics

## Characteristics

Moat erosion should be:

- Observable and mechanism-linked
- Distinct from ordinary cyclical noise where possible
- Time-stamped and evidence-backed
- Escalated when multiple signals cluster
- Separated from price volatility alone
- Explicit about confidence

## What It Is Not

Moat erosion is not:

- A short-term stock price decline
- Quality deterioration in general (BQ-012)
- Ordinary seasonality
- Proof of immediate business failure
- A valuation result
- An automatic sell recommendation

---

## Evidence Requirements

- Narrowing cost or price gaps
- Rising customer churn or multihoming
- Successful rival imitation or substitution
- Regulatory barrier weakening
- Falling ROIC despite prior moat claims
- Distribution or ecosystem displacement

## Confidence Drivers
